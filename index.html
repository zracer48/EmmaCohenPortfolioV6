Emma Cohen

Emma is a 17-year-old artist from Alexandria, Virginia whose work is rooted in close observation, childhood wonder, femininity, beauty, growth, and the small moments people often pass by.

Her earliest artistic lessons came from drawing beside her grandmother, who would tell her to “squint at it” before putting pencil to paper. What began as a lesson in noticing shadow, light, and form became a way of seeing the world: slow down, look again, and find what might have been missed the first time.

Through AP Art, Emma learned to move beyond perfectionism and comparison and trust the perspective that makes her work distinctly her own. Her portfolio repeatedly returns to family, wildflowers, childhood, transformation, and quiet expressions of wonder.

Fragments of Becoming

A study of childhood wonder, safety, innocence, femininity, and the transition toward womanhood through photography, collage, and layered visual symbolism.

Stage 01 · Reference Photography

Capturing childhood wonder

For my process, I photographed my little sister’s best friend to capture the youth and wonder of childhood. I used the bunny and flowers as symbols of innocence, beauty, and femininity, while the light shining down represents the hope and possibility that define early life. By exploring multiple angles of the figure and props, I was able to select the most compelling composition for my piece.

Stage 02 · Exploratory Draft

Testing composition and texture

This piece began as an exploratory draft, where I arranged materials without adhering them to allow for flexibility. I experimented with paper textures and colors to evoke childhood wonder while developing a more mature, cohesive composition. The placement of each figure reflects themes of femininity and beauty through balance and softness.

Stage 03 · Refinement

Securing and simplifying

This second stage marks when I secured the materials to the paper. I refined the composition by removing the ribbon, adjusting the placement of images, and enhancing the layering to create a more cohesive and resolved final piece.

Stage 04 · Final Selections

Safety carried forward

These two images are the final selections from my earlier set. I chose the child hugging the bunny to symbolize safety and innocence, and the image of her running to suggest carrying that sense of security forward. In my drawing, this progression reflects the transformation into womanhood, capturing femininity as both beautiful and complex, while preserving a sense of childhood wonder.

Growing in Tandem

A layered exploration of childhood playfulness, femininity, texture, value, and the relationship between drawing and decorative material.

Stage 01 · Background Study

Scratchboard, texture, and white space

While developing the background of my piece, I experimented with floral decorative elements to determine whether they complemented the composition. I used scratchboard techniques with black paper, incorporating cross-hatching and stippling to achieve the desired texture and depth. Throughout this process, I struggled with effectively using the white space to allow the image to fully emerge.

Stage 02 · Material Experimentation

Childhood textiles and playfulness

For this stage of my process, I experimented with different materials such as ribbon for the girls’ clothing, floral cotton swabs for added detail, and a coquette-style paper bag for the background. I incorporated a variety of textures throughout the piece to emphasize textiles associated with childhood playfulness, while also echoing the ribbon details present in the original reference image.

Stage 03 · Early Value Studies

Learning from overly dark values

This row was the first couple of sketches I did where I shaded a bit too dark in the face and messed up my values just a bit.

Stage 04 · Value Correction

Softening the face and arms

In these next stages, I recognized that my shading was too dark and used a kneaded eraser to lift graphite from the face and arms, creating a more natural and realistic effect. I also used image transfer techniques to refine the shadows and better capture the depth of the original reference.

Stage 05 · Final Refinement

Resolving the face and background

In the final stages of my piece, I refined the face by softening the darker values and resolved the background. The variety of flowers represents the youth and playfulness of childhood, while their color and diversity emphasize beauty and femininity.

A World Within and Beyond

A transition from the vibrancy of childhood into a more complicated reality, using flowers, distressed paper, collage, and fragmented imagery.

Stage 01 · Exploration

Finding the right medium

At the start of this process, I sketched the image and explored the themes behind the piece. Unsure of which medium would best convey the emotion, I experimented with different materials, including watercolor, to develop the final direction and mood of the work.

Stage 02 · Background Development

Distressing, fraying, and collage

I then developed the background by distressing canvas paper and fraying the edges to add depth and symbolism. I incorporated collage elements to unify the color palette, which later inspired the visual direction of my other pieces.

Stage 03 · Visual Progression

When childhood begins to fade

Throughout my process, I used vibrant floral colors to represent the beauty and excitement of childhood. As the composition progresses, the shaded figure reflects the moment youth begins to fade, revealing a more complicated reality. This shift continues as she encounters a hollow, fragmented sketch of the world, suggesting that adulthood is not the fairytale she once imagined. I also burned the edges of the canvas paper to create the effect of reality bleeding into her sense of childhood innocence.

Chapters of Life

A visual progression through childhood, young adulthood, middle age, and later life, connecting maturity, femininity, identity, and symbolic flowers.

Stage 01 · Concept & References

Life stages through paint swatches

I developed the concept for this piece by studying paint swatches, using their segmented structure to represent different stages of life: childhood, young adulthood, middle age, and later life. Each section becomes a visual marker of growth and transition, reflecting the progression of maturity over time. I used my sisters as references for the figures, grounding the work in personal experience while exploring themes of femininity, identity, and the evolving perception of beauty throughout life.

Stage 02 · Process Development

Flowers as markers of growth

I drew inspiration from paint swatches and images of my sisters to create this piece, cutting the paper to mirror the segmented structure of a swatch. This format reflects the beauty of growth and the progression of maturity, emphasizing how childhood holds a sense of openness and possibility for what lies ahead. I paired each figure with a symbolic flower to represent different stages of life: the daisy for childhood, the cherry blossom for young adulthood, the rose for middle age, and the lotus for later life—each highlighting evolving ideas of beauty and femininity over time.
